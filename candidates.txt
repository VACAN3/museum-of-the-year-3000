Museum of the Year 3000 — candidate labels (3)
Rules followed: one twist only, punch on the last word/sentence, anchor on
one specific mundane object, never give the joke away in the opening, deadpan
museum-label voice, future curators misreading what the object was for.

1. [CHOSEN] Group Chat, c. 2025. Seventeen humans shared a single
   conversation for nine years. Fourteen of them never spoke. All
   seventeen read everything.
   (143 chars)
   Why it wins: freshest object of the three (the bounty names the group
   chat, yet almost every entrant will reach for the fork/key first); the
   misreading is the museum treating a chat log as a social ritual; the
   punch lands on the very last word — everybody silently watched
   everything. True, absurd, and instantly recognisable on first read.

2. Parking Meter, c. 2024. Citizens fed this box coins to rent the ground
   their car was already standing on. The box kept the coins.
   (129 chars)
   Runner-up: clean single twist (paying rent on ground you already occupy)
   and a good last-sentence punch ("The box kept the coins"). Loses to #1
   because the joke is slightly more explainable — the reader can see the
   punch coming one clause earlier.

3. House Key, c. 2020. A small metal tooth that opened a family home. It
   was copied freely, lost constantly, and hidden under a rock by the
   front door.
   (148 chars)
   Cut: three escalating observations instead of one twist, so the laugh
   is diluted; "hidden under a rock" is the most obvious first joke about
   a house key, and the judge's criterion 5 punishes the obvious angle.
